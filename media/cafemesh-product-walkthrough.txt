Welcome to CaféMesh AI: your café experience, intelligently connected. CaféMesh brings the guest planning a visit, the café team serving them, and the owner improving the business into one connected experience. This public demo is live on Google Cloud at cafemesh dot stackclimb dot com.

Here is a realistic request: “I have forty minutes. I am vegetarian and severely allergic to peanuts. I want a cold drink under three hundred and fifty rupees, and somewhere quiet to work.” The app extracts those stated constraints and keeps them visible. Allergies are never learned from taste feedback or weakened by the model.

Discovery is powered by live Google Places listings and a live Google Routes walking estimate. The default demo origin is Indiranagar Metro Station in Bengaluru. A visitor can enter another landmark or, by pressing “Use my location,” grant one-time browser location access for that search. CaféMesh sends the coordinates to its server for Places and Routes, displays a location label, and does not save the coordinates. A Maps listing is not proof that a café participates in CaféMesh; café menus, inventory, seating, and orders in this demo remain synthetic.

The demo menu now includes hot drinks such as espresso, Americano, cappuccino, latte, and masala chai as well as cold beverages. Search uses exact menu names and a small transparent synonym map, then deterministic filters apply budget, temperature, dietary, allergy, stock, safety evidence, and timing constraints. It is structured lexical retrieval, not semantic search or RAG. An unsupported request returns a clear no-match result instead of a made-up drink.

The experience is powered by four Google ADK agents: Concierge coordinates, TasteSafety retrieves menu evidence, VisitOrder checks visit context, and OpsOwner reads operational and business facts. Gemini runs through Vertex AI when available. For allergy requests, free-form model prose is withheld if exact constraints could drift; the structured records and deterministic eligibility checks remain visible. Unknown or stale cross-contact evidence goes to staff review, and CaféMesh never promises that a menu item is allergy-safe.

When current records permit, the guest reviews the exact item, modifications, price, and timing before explicitly confirming a simulated order. The same order appears in the Operations queue. Staff can update its demo status and enter a measured preparation time. A manager can accept or reject a grounded suggestion; that decision is audited separately and never pretends to dispatch or reassign staff.

Operations also shows synthetic queue and occupancy signals, low-stock alerts, safety reviews, agent and tool outcomes, latency, provider status, errors, and review candidates. These records come from the shared demo state. The dashboard is useful operational instrumentation, not a production service-level objective or a connection to a real café floor.

Owner Intelligence calculates metrics from those same shared orders and events: order count and value, wait, repeat activity, recommendation conversion, stockouts, feedback, and safety escalations. It states the reporting window, shows insufficient data when records are missing, and avoids claiming that correlation proves causation.

After a visit, feedback can remember oat milk, lower sweetness, no caramel, and quiet seating. The customer can see those preference changes shape a later recommendation and reset them. Safety restrictions are stored separately and are not changed by this learning loop.

The public experience is hosted on Google Cloud Run behind the CaféMesh Stackclimb domain. Google Sign-In protects the Operations and Owner reviewer views; the consent screen is still in Testing, so access is limited to listed testers. Vertex AI with ADK, Places, Routes, Firestore-backed synthetic demo state, and the saved Despina Indian-English narration are connected. Café business data and actions are simulations.

For a real café pilot, CaféMesh needs tenant-isolated onboarding and administration, reviewed menu and safety records, staff roles, transactional inventory, consent and data controls, cancellations, notifications, backups, and production monitoring. Operations can grow into station workload, shift handover, reservations, room feedback, and service recovery; owners can gain verified menu economics and multi-location views. Document RAG is a considered next step, not a feature we claim today: it can retrieve long café procedures and accessibility or policy documents once there is a governed, versioned corpus, while live prices, allergens, and stock remain structured facts. Future journeys include meet-halfway discovery, group planning, arrival-aware preparation, POS, payments, loyalty, multilingual voice, forecasting, BigQuery analytics, and evaluated recommendation models.

CaféMesh connects a guest’s choices to the team’s service, the owner’s insight, and a more helpful return visit. Evidence stays visible, people keep control of important actions, and every capability is described at the stage it has actually reached.
